Redbird workshop

In this workshop, you build an accessibility fix for a user on Redbird, a practice online store, using the AI for Accessibility Toolkit.

  1. Try the toolkit as one of four shoppers.
  2. Learn the framework: ability profiles, adaptations and verification.
  3. Build a fix, following two examples.
  4. Build your own for the person your group picked.
  5. Test it and share it.

Steps 1 and 2 happen during the morning's toolkit intro and framework sessions, and steps 3 to 5 during the afternoon's building sessions.

The toolkit panel

Each store link in this guide opens Redbird with the toolkit's panel next to it. The panel has four tabs:

  • Profiles is where you make your own profile, or pick someone to shop as. It shows which adapters are on for them.
  • Adapter Library has a switch for every adapter.
  • In Build your adapter, you describe a change and get a fix for it: a skill made of the toolkit's adapters, or a new adapter that AI writes.
  • Audit finds the accessibility problems on the page, and can fix them all at once.

Redbird's built-in problems

We built 36 accessibility problems into Redbird on purpose, the same kinds that real shopping sites have. For example, the color choices have no names a screen reader can say, delivery says "5–7 business days" instead of a date, and countdowns start over every time you visit.

Tip

If the store gets into a weird state, you can reset it. Your saved fixes are kept.
