Redbird workshop

Build an accessibility fix for one person's need on Redbird, a practice online store, using the AI for Accessibility Toolkit. You only need a browser, and most people build their fix without writing code.

What you'll build

By the end of the day, you'll have a working fix on Redbird for the need your group chose, and a link to show it. There are two ways to make one:

  • A skill turns on adapters the toolkit already has, for one need. You describe the need, and AI picks the adapters. For example, a skill for Mark can stop the moving deals bar and hide "Only 2 left in stock".
  • An adapter is new code that changes the page. You describe the fix in plain words, and AI writes the code. For example, an adapter can read the star ratings out to a screen reader as "4.6 out of 5 stars".

The other sessions (refining the scenario, the card game, storyboarding and the premortem) happen on paper. Bring what you decide there to the building session: who you're building for, what to change on the page, and how you'll check that it worked.

Why a practice store

Most of the web still doesn't work well for people with disabilities. In 2026, WebAIM found accessibility failures on 95.9% of the top million home pages (The WebAIM Million (opens in a new tab)). Usually these stay broken until the site's owner fixes them. Instead, the AI for Accessibility Toolkit (opens in a new tab) changes the page in the person's own browser.

Redbird has 36 problems like the ones on real shopping sites, built in on purpose. For example, the color choices have no names a screen reader can say, delivery says "5–7 business days" instead of a date, and a protection plan is already ticked when you buy. You can turn each problem on or off, so you can see exactly what a fix changes. Nothing here buys anything or reaches a real person.

The Workshop panel

Store links in these guides open the Workshop panel on the side of the store. It has four tabs:

  • Setup: pick who you're shopping as, and switch adapters on and off.
  • Build: make a skill, or describe a fix for AI to write.
  • Changes: everything the adapters changed, before and after, and what a screen reader hears.
  • Checks: the toolkit's checks, and badges on the problems built into the page.

To make the panel smaller, press the – button at its top right. To move it to the other side of the page, press the arrow.

Tip

If the store gets into a weird state, reset the store (opens in a new tab). This turns the problems back on, stops the adapters and empties the cart. Your skills and your adapter are kept.
